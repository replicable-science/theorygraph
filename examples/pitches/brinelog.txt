Good afternoon. Oyster farmers lose a whole season of income when the water turns on them, and most of them find out after the oysters are already dead. Last August a farm in Maine lost four hundred thousand oysters in two days because of a low oxygen event that nobody saw coming. That's why we built Brinelog. Brinelog is a floating sensor the size of a coffee can that measures oxygen, salinity and temperature every minute and texts the farmer when a reading crosses a danger line. The sensor runs on a small solar panel, so a farmer never has to service it, and we sell it as a subscription for forty dollars a month per sensor. We tested forty sensors on six farms in Maine and Nova Scotia over the 2024 season. Our alerts came on average two hours and ten minutes before the first oyster losses, and on the three farms that acted on them, losses were cut by about sixty percent. Insurers care about this. We have a letter of intent from a marine insurer that would discount premiums by up to ten percent for farms that use Brinelog, which we think could pay for the subscription twice over. Our plan is to sell through the state aquaculture extension offices, because every oyster farmer in the country already trusts and calls them. We expect to reach three hundred farms next year. Longer term we hope the same sensor can serve shrimp and salmon farms, although we have not tested that. There are about four thousand shellfish farms in the United States, and our team has built marine sensors for ten years. We are raising a million dollars to build the next two hundred units. Thank you.
